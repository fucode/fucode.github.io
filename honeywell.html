COMPANY UNDERSTANDING

What Does Honeywell Do?

In simple terms, Honeywell Technologies makes the hardware, software, sensors, control systems and process technologies that help buildings, factories and industrial operations run more automatically, safely and efficiently.

SIMPLE MENTAL MODEL

Sense → Control → Automate → Optimize

A sensor detects what is happening → a control system decides what should happen → automation makes it happen → software and data help the business monitor and optimize the operation.

Simple explanation: Honeywell doesn't just automate industrial plants — it also provides specialized technologies used inside major industrial processes.
CONNECTION TO THE ROLE

Why This Matters to an S/4 Product Owner

Honeywell builds complex physical products and industrial technologies. Behind those products are thousands of materials, components, suppliers, engineering changes, manufacturing processes and enterprise transactions.

01

Product Data

Products require accurate material, engineering and manufacturing information.

02

Materials

Components need to be sourced, planned, purchased, tracked and available.

03

Manufacturing

Engineering definitions must eventually become something factories can actually build.

04

Enterprise Scale

Standardized processes and data help complex global operations execute consistently.

MY CONNECTION

S/4HANA becomes part of the enterprise backbone.

My interest in this role is the connection between innovation and execution. Honeywell can design innovative products, but those designs eventually need accurate materials, BOMs, suppliers, manufacturing information and business processes. As Product Owner, I would help translate those business needs into prioritized S/4 capabilities the Agile team can deliver.

VALUE STREAM

Understanding Innovation to Order

My starting point would be understanding how an idea or product change progresses from engineering into the enterprise data and processes required for manufacturing and execution.

1. Innovation / Business Need
Identify the customer, product, engineering or market need.
2. Product & Engineering Definition
Define product requirements, specifications and product structure.
3. Material Master & Product Data
Establish the enterprise data required to represent the product and its components.
4. Bill of Materials
Define which components and quantities are required to build the product.
5. Engineering Change Control
Manage changes to designs, components and specifications through controlled approvals and traceability.
6. Material & Supply Readiness
Ensure components can be sourced, purchased and made available when needed.
7. Manufacturing Readiness
Confirm that product data and operational requirements are ready for manufacturing.
8. Order / Production Execution
Move the approved product definition into operational execution.

My Product Owner Operating Model

My job is to create the bridge between business strategy, subject-matter expertise and technical execution.

01

Discover

Interview SMEs, users, process owners and technical teams. Understand current-state workflows and pain points.

02

Define

Translate business needs into features, stories, requirements and measurable acceptance criteria.

03

Prioritize

Order work according to business value, urgency, risk, dependencies and delivery impact.

04

Validate

Review completed functionality, support UAT, accept stories and determine whether the intended outcome was achieved.

From Strategy to Team Backlog

One of my primary responsibilities would be converting product strategy into work an Agile team can understand, estimate, build and test.

LEVEL 1

Vision

Standardize and modernize the enterprise Innovation-to-Order process.

LEVEL 2

Feature

Improve creation and validation of material master data for new product introduction.

LEVEL 3

Story

Enable a Product Engineer to create required material information using standardized fields and validation.

LEVEL 4

Acceptance

Validate required data, permissions, business rules, exceptions and downstream availability.

Example: Business Need → User Story

A Product Owner should be able to take a broad business problem and progressively translate it into something the delivery team can build and validate.

SAMPLE USER STORY

Material Master Validation

As a Product Engineer,
I want required product and material information validated before submission,
so that incomplete or inaccurate master data does not create downstream manufacturing or procurement issues.

Example Acceptance Criteria
Given I am an authorized Product Engineer When I create a new material Then all required fields must be displayed And required fields must be completed before submission And invalid values must generate a clear validation message And successfully created material data must be available to authorized downstream processes And the transaction must maintain required audit information

How I Would Prioritize the Backlog

Not every request should automatically become the next story. Prioritization requires understanding both business value and delivery impact.

Business Value

Customer impact, operational efficiency, cost reduction, compliance and strategic alignment.

Delivery Impact

Dependencies, complexity, technical enablement, sequencing and team capacity.

Risk & Urgency

Production risk, regulatory requirements, defects, deadlines and blocked downstream capabilities.

SCALED AGILE DELIVERY

SAFe & PI Planning

PI Planning is where product strategy becomes coordinated execution. My responsibility is to arrive with enough clarity around priorities, dependencies and upcoming work for the team to plan effectively.

Before PI Planning

  • Review roadmap and program priorities
  • Understand upcoming features
  • Break features into team-level work
  • Refine high-priority stories
  • Identify dependencies
  • Review risks and assumptions
  • Ensure sufficient backlog readiness

During PI Planning

  • Support feature decomposition
  • Clarify functional requirements
  • Align work with capacity
  • Coordinate dependencies
  • Contribute to team PI objectives
  • Surface risks early
  • Negotiate scope when necessary

ACTION PLAN

My First 90 Days

My objective would not be to walk in and immediately redesign Honeywell's process. I would first learn the environment, then take ownership of execution, and finally use delivery data and stakeholder feedback to identify improvements.

DAYS 1–30

Learn & Map

Build the business, SAP and organizational context required to make informed backlog decisions.

  • Meet Product Manager
  • Meet Scrum Master
  • Meet business process owners
  • Meet SMEs
  • Meet SAP functional leads
  • Meet technical leads
  • Understand the ART structure
  • Review the S/4 roadmap
  • Review current PI objectives
  • Review the Jira backlog
  • Map the I2O workflow
  • Understand major integrations
  • Learn Material Master and BOM processes
  • Review defects and pain points
Deliverables
  • Stakeholder map
  • Current-state process map
  • System / dependency map
  • Backlog health assessment
  • Pain-point inventory
DAYS 31–60

Execute & Own

Transition from learning the environment to actively owning Product Owner execution.

  • Own assigned Team Backlog
  • Facilitate refinement
  • Improve user stories
  • Define acceptance criteria
  • Prioritize with Product Management
  • Support sprint planning
  • Provide requirement clarification
  • Track dependencies
  • Track blockers
  • Validate completed stories
  • Support UAT
  • Prepare upcoming PI work
  • Maintain N+2 / N+3 readiness
Deliverables
  • Prioritized Team Backlog
  • Refined upcoming stories
  • Dependency tracker
  • UAT / acceptance approach
  • Backlog readiness view
DAYS 61–90

Optimize & Add Value

Move beyond managing stories and begin improving how the team delivers business outcomes.

  • Analyze recurring pain points
  • Improve backlog quality
  • Reduce requirement ambiguity
  • Improve dependency visibility
  • Identify standardization opportunities
  • Identify manual process opportunities
  • Analyze recurring defects
  • Measure delivery predictability
  • Improve stakeholder feedback loops
  • Contribute confidently to PI Planning
  • Recommend future backlog opportunities
  • Connect delivery metrics to outcomes
Deliverables
  • Backlog health improvements
  • Process improvement recommendations
  • KPI baseline
  • Prioritized improvement opportunities
  • Next-PI recommendations

How I Would Measure Success

Story completion alone does not prove the transformation is successful. I would evaluate both Agile delivery health and business outcomes.

Cycle Time

Time required to move priority work through delivery.

Backlog Readiness

Percentage of upcoming work meeting readiness standards.

Defects

Escaped defects and recurring functional issues.

PI Predictability

Delivery against planned PI objectives.

Data Quality

Completeness and accuracy of critical product data.

Rework

Work repeated because of incomplete requirements or data.

Process Time

Time required to complete important business process steps.

Adoption

Successful usage of standardized S/4 capabilities.

How I Would Add Value

Business → Technology Translator

Understand the business problem first and translate it into requirements technical teams can execute without losing the original business intent.

Execution Discipline

Maintain a healthy, prioritized backlog and ensure upcoming work has enough clarity to support predictable delivery.

Stakeholder Alignment

Create a consistent feedback loop between Product Management, SMEs, process owners, developers, testers and other Product Owners.

Outcome Focus

A story reaching Done is not the final measure of success. I want to know whether the delivered capability actually improved the underlying business process.

APPENDIX B

SAP S/4HANA & Innovation-to-Order Glossary

Searchable quick-reference definitions for terminology across product, engineering, manufacturing, supply chain and SAP.

ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning

Enterprise software connecting major business functions such as manufacturing, procurement, inventory, finance, supply chain and sales.

SAP

A major enterprise software company whose systems help organizations manage business processes and enterprise data.

SAP S/4HANA

SAP's modern ERP platform connecting enterprise processes including finance, procurement, manufacturing, supply chain and master data.

HANA

SAP's in-memory database technology underlying S/4HANA.

SAP Fiori

SAP's modern role-based user experience used to interact with SAP business processes.

I2O — Innovation to Order

A value stream connecting product innovation and engineering to the enterprise information and processes required to manufacture and operationalize a product.

NPI — New Product Introduction

The process of moving a newly designed product from development toward manufacturing and market readiness.

Master Data

Core enterprise information reused across multiple transactions and processes, such as materials, suppliers, customers and products.

Material

An item managed within SAP, such as a raw material, component, semi-finished item or finished product.

Material Master

The central SAP record containing information about a material used across procurement, inventory, manufacturing and other processes.

Simple example: A sensor used in a Honeywell product may have a material number, description, unit of measure, procurement data, planning information and other attributes.
BOM — Bill of Materials

The structured list of components and quantities required to build a product.

Think of it as: The recipe for the product.
EBOM — Engineering Bill of Materials

The product structure representing how engineering designed the product.

MBOM — Manufacturing Bill of Materials

The product structure organized around how the product will actually be manufactured.

Routing

The sequence of manufacturing operations or work steps required to produce a product.

Think of it as: BOM tells us what we need. Routing tells us how we build it.
Work Center

The machine, production line, labor group or location where a manufacturing operation is performed.

MRP — Material Requirements Planning

Planning logic used to determine what materials are required, how many are needed and when they are needed.

Think of it as: Do we have the parts needed to build what we plan to build?
Procurement

The business process used to obtain goods and services from suppliers.

Purchase Requisition

An internal request indicating that a material or service needs to be purchased.

Purchase Order

A formal order sent to a supplier requesting materials or services.

Inventory

Materials or products physically available within the organization or supply chain.

Production Order

An SAP object authorizing and controlling production of a specified quantity of a product.

Engineering Change Management

The controlled process for modifying product designs, components, specifications or structures while maintaining approvals and traceability.

ECM — Engineering Change Management

Common abbreviation for the process of controlling and tracking engineering changes.

PLM — Product Lifecycle Management

Systems and processes used to manage product information from concept and engineering through manufacturing, maintenance and retirement.

SAP PP — Production Planning

SAP functionality supporting production planning, manufacturing requirements and execution.

SAP MM — Materials Management

SAP functionality supporting materials, procurement, inventory and related processes.

SAP SD — Sales & Distribution

SAP functionality supporting sales orders, pricing, shipping and customer fulfillment.

SAP FI — Financial Accounting

SAP functionality supporting financial accounting, transactions and reporting.

SAP CO — Controlling

SAP functionality supporting internal cost management, profitability analysis and management accounting.

Data Migration

Moving business data from legacy systems into a new environment such as S/4HANA.

Data Quality

The completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness and usability of enterprise data.

Integration

A connection allowing systems or applications to exchange data and coordinate business processes.

API — Application Programming Interface

A defined method allowing software systems to communicate and exchange information.

IDoc — Intermediate Document

A structured SAP message format commonly used to exchange business information between SAP and other systems.

Batch Processing

Processing transactions or data together at scheduled intervals rather than immediately.

Real-Time Integration

Exchanging and processing information immediately or near-immediately when a business event occurs.

UAT — User Acceptance Testing

Business-user validation confirming that delivered functionality meets requirements and supports the intended process.

Cutover

The coordinated activities required to transition from the old system or process to the new production solution.

Go-Live

The point when the new system or capability becomes available for production use.

Hypercare

A period of elevated support immediately following go-live to identify and resolve production issues quickly.

APPENDIX C

Product Owner / Agile / SAFe Glossary

PO — Product Owner

Owns and prioritizes the Team Backlog and provides day-to-day clarification to the Agile team.

PM — Product Manager

Owns broader product strategy, roadmap and feature-level priorities.

SAFe — Scaled Agile Framework

An approach for coordinating Agile delivery across multiple teams and larger enterprise initiatives.

ART — Agile Release Train

A long-lived group of Agile teams working together toward a shared product or solution mission.

PI — Planning Interval

A larger SAFe delivery timebox containing multiple iterations or sprints.

PI Planning

A collaborative planning event where teams align on priorities, objectives, capacity, risks and dependencies for the upcoming PI.

Team Backlog

The prioritized collection of stories, defects and enablers owned by the Product Owner for the Agile team.

Feature

A larger piece of functionality or business value that can be decomposed into team-level stories.

User Story

A small, testable description of functionality from the perspective of a user or stakeholder.

Acceptance Criteria

Specific conditions that must be satisfied for a story to meet its expected business behavior.

Enabler

Technical, architectural or infrastructure work needed to support future business functionality.

Dependency

Work that relies on another team, system, capability or activity before it can be completed.

Definition of Ready

Agreed criteria indicating that work contains enough information and clarity to enter development.

Definition of Done

Agreed criteria defining what must be completed before work can be considered finished.

Backlog Refinement

Ongoing activity where upcoming work is clarified, decomposed, estimated and prepared for future delivery.

Iteration / Sprint

A short delivery cycle during which the team builds, tests and demonstrates working functionality.

PI Objective

A concise statement describing the business or technical outcome a team intends to accomplish during the PI.

Business Value

The expected benefit delivered by a capability, such as revenue, efficiency, risk reduction, compliance or customer impact.
