01 / Overview

Treating data as a product—not simply as a technical asset.

A Data Product Owner applies product-management principles to enterprise data. The goal is to ensure data is useful, understandable, trusted, governed and accessible to the consumers who need it.

In a large bank, data moves through many systems and supports reporting, customer experiences, risk management, regulatory requirements, operations, analytics and decision-making.

The Data Product Owner helps connect those business needs to engineering and governance requirements while maintaining a prioritized backlog and measurable product outcomes.

Product
Business Value

Understand consumers, prioritize use cases and create a roadmap for the data product.

10 / Data Quality

Good data must be fit for its intended purpose.

Data quality is broader than asking whether a field contains a value. Different dimensions measure different types of reliability.

Accuracy

Does the data correctly represent the real-world value?

Completeness

Are required records and attributes populated?

Consistency

Does the information agree across systems?

Timeliness

Is the information available when consumers need it?

Validity

Does the data follow required formats and business rules?

Uniqueness

Are duplicate records controlled appropriately?

12 / Data Engineering Concepts

Movement, orchestration, transformation, translation & consumption.

Use the MSOW / Pega reporting flow as a practical way to remember how these concepts differ. The overall process is a data pipeline, while different activities within that pipeline perform different functions.

MSOW / Pega Analytics Example

A simplified example based on the MSOW reporting flow: operational Pega data is processed into a structure that can support analytics and Power BI reporting.

Pega / MSOW Operational case data, including data stored in the Pega BLOB
AutoSys Schedules / orchestrates data-processing jobs
Transform Extract, parse, restructure and prepare Pega data
Analytics Database Structured data available for analytics
Power BI Analytics team consumes the data for reporting
Memory trick: AutoSys helps orchestrate the process, the pipeline moves the data, transformation makes the Pega data usable, translation explains what coded values mean, and Power BI consumes the resulting data.
Where?

Data Movement

Moving data from one system, database or environment to another.

MSOW Example:
Moving Pega / MSOW data into an analytics database.
When & In What Order?

Orchestration

Coordinating and scheduling the jobs or processes that make up the data pipeline.

MSOW Example:
AutoSys schedules or coordinates the jobs involved in moving and processing the Pega data.
What Does It Become?

Transformation

Changing the structure, format or values of data so it becomes usable for its intended purpose.

MSOW Example:
Parsing data from a Pega BLOB and restructuring attributes into usable database fields or columns for analytics.
What Does It Mean?

Translation

Mapping one representation of a value to another representation while preserving the underlying business meaning.

Simple Examples:
A → Active
I → Inactive

Illustrative risk example:
Risk Code 5 → High Risk
Where Does It Live?

Analytics Storage

Prepared data is stored in a structured environment where it can be queried and used by downstream consumers.

MSOW Example:
Transformed data is made available in an analytics database for reporting and analysis.
Who Uses It?

Data Consumption

Applications, analysts, dashboards, reports or models use prepared data to produce business value.

MSOW Example:
The analytics team consumes the prepared data through Power BI to build reports and dashboards.
Remember This

Five questions.

Instead of memorizing technical definitions, associate each concept with one question.

Concept Question Your Example
Movement Where is it going? Pega → Analytics DB
Orchestration When / in what order? AutoSys coordinates the jobs
Translation What does it mean? A → Active
Transformation What does it become? Pega BLOB → structured fields
Consumption Who uses it? Analytics DB → Power BI
Important Distinction

Translation vs. Transformation

Translation can occur as part of a broader transformation, but the terms emphasize different things.

Translation: "What does this value mean?"

Transformation: "What do I need to do to this data?"

21 / Interview Prep

Explain the concepts clearly without sounding overly technical.

These are concise ways to frame the concepts during a Product Owner or Data Product interview.

What is a Data Product Owner?

Product ownership applied to data.

A Data Product Owner treats data as a product. I would focus on understanding the consumers and business outcomes, prioritizing the backlog, and partnering with engineering, architecture and governance to make sure the data is accessible, understandable, reliable and properly governed.

What is data governance?

Accountability and trust.

Data governance establishes the ownership, definitions, standards and controls around enterprise data. It helps answer who owns the data, what it means, where it came from, who can access it and whether it meets expected quality standards.

What is data lineage?

Trace the data from source to consumer.

Data lineage shows how data travels from the original source through ingestion and transformations to the final report, application or other consumer. It is especially useful for impact analysis, troubleshooting and governance.

What is metadata?

Context about the data.

Metadata is data about data. It provides context such as the business definition, technical field name, datatype, source, owner, classification and other information needed to understand and use the data correctly.

Translation vs. transformation?

Meaning vs. change.

I remember translation as asking what a source value means. For example, a coded value such as A may translate to Active. Transformation is broader and changes the structure, format or value of data. An example would be extracting information from a Pega BLOB and restructuring it into fields that can be used in an analytics database.

How technical should a Data PO be?

Technical enough to connect the dots.

I do not need to replace the data engineer or architect, but I need enough technical understanding to discuss source systems, tables, APIs, pipelines, transformations, lineage, dependencies and quality so I can translate between business and technical teams.

22 / Mental Model

The six questions to ask about any data product.

If you can answer these questions, you can usually understand the product well enough to have a productive conversation with business, governance and engineering teams.

Data Product Owner Checklist

  1. Where does the data come from?
    Identify the authoritative source and upstream systems.
  2. How does it get here?
    Understand ingestion, movement and interfaces.
  3. What happens to it?
    Understand transformation and business rules.
  4. What does it mean?
    Understand translation, metadata, definitions and ownership.
  5. Can we trust it?
    Understand quality rules, controls and monitoring.
  6. Who uses it and why?
    Understand consumers and the business outcome.
