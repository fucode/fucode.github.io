01 • Business Overview

Risk Profile Assessment (RPA)

Risk Profile Assessment supported Wells Fargo Merchant Services Credit and Risk teams in evaluating merchants and determining whether the merchant's overall risk profile and credit exposure remained acceptable to the bank.

Merchant Assessment

Brought merchant information, processing activity, risk indicators, exposure, and relationship information together for underwriting review.

Risk Evaluation

Credit Officers and Underwriters evaluated merchant exposure, risk rating, processing activity, collateral, and other relevant risk factors.

Credit Decision Support

RPA organized the information, documentation, analysis, and approvals required to support merchant credit-risk decisions and case resolution.

02 • Application Architecture

MSOW vs. MSCD

A key architectural concept was the separation between MSOW, which provided the Pega user interface and workflow, and MSCD, which served as an upstream merchant-data application and API provider.

Upstream / Backend

MSCD

MSCD functioned as an upstream Merchant Services application and data store supporting merchant-data processing, storage, and analysis.

Key Responsibilities
  • Receive merchant-related data from upstream sources.
  • Store merchant information.
  • Support analysis used for periodic RPA reviews.
  • Receive or process alert-related information that could contribute to merchant reviews.
  • Provide merchant information and functionality through APIs.
  • Serve as an upstream system for MSOW.
  • Support approximately 10 years of data retention.
Upstream Data Store API Provider Merchant Data
UI / Workflow

MSOW

MSOW was the Pega-based application providing the user interface, case-management experience, and workflow used by Merchant Services users to perform the Risk Profile Assessment.

Key Responsibilities
  • Consume APIs provided by MSCD.
  • Present merchant information to users.
  • Create and manage RPA cases.
  • Guide users through Pega stages and process steps.
  • Support underwriting analysis.
  • Support document-related processes.
  • Support approval and case-resolution workflows.
  • Maintain its own application and case data.
  • Support approximately 3 years of data retention.

04 • Business Process

RPA Workflow

The RPA case guided the Credit Officer or Underwriter through the information and activities required to evaluate a merchant.

Important

This is a conceptual view of the RPA process for study purposes. Not every activity necessarily occurred in a strict sequential order.

Pega Terminology

A Pega case progresses through Stages. Each stage can contain one or more Processes and Steps that guide the user toward case completion.

1
RPA Case Initiation

An RPA case could originate from a periodic merchant review or from risk / alert-related activity requiring merchant evaluation.

2
Merchant Review

The underwriter reviewed merchant profile information, merchant hierarchy, processing activity, and other relevant information.

3
Risk Analysis

The Credit Officer analyzed merchant risk indicators, processing behavior, exposure, risk rating, and other relevant factors.

4
Gather Additional Information

If additional information was needed, the underwriter could contact other groups, including the merchant's Relationship Manager.

5
Supporting Documents

Supporting documentation could be uploaded and used as part of the RPA assessment.

6
Wells Fargo Relationship Review

The underwriter could enter or review other products and services the merchant had with Wells Fargo to provide additional relationship context.

7
Collateral

Collateral could be evaluated as part of the merchant's overall credit-risk assessment.

8
Credit Amount / Exposure

The assessment considered the amount of processing-related credit exposure the bank was willing to assume for the merchant.

9
Concurrence / Approval

When additional approval was required, the workflow supported identifying and routing the case to the appropriate approver.

10
Case Resolution

After analysis, documentation, and required approvals were completed, the RPA case could move toward final resolution.

08 • Data Concepts

Architecture & Data Terminology

Common terminology used to describe how merchant data moved between the systems supporting the RPA process.

Term How It Applied
Data Source The original system or provider from which merchant information originated.
Data Sourcing Identifying and obtaining the merchant information required by the business process.
Data Movement Moving information from one system or platform to another.
Data Ingestion Bringing merchant information from source systems into MSCD.
Data Store A system or database responsible for storing data. MSCD served as a merchant-data store.
Upstream System A system that provides data or functionality to another system. MSCD was upstream of MSOW.
Downstream System A system that receives or consumes information from an upstream system. MSOW consumed information from MSCD.
API Provider The application exposing functionality or data through an API. MSCD served as an API provider.
API Consumer The application calling an API and using the returned data or functionality. MSOW served as an API consumer.
Transformation Changing the structure, format, or values of data so that it meets the requirements of another application or business process.
Translation / Mapping Mapping fields, values, or representations between systems so the receiving system interprets the information correctly.
Data Lineage Understanding where data originated, how it moved, how it changed, and which applications consumed it.
Data Governance Policies and controls governing data ownership, quality, retention, access, and appropriate use.

11 • Interview Preparation

How I Explain RPA

A concise way to explain the project during a Product Owner or Business Analyst interview.

At Wells Fargo Merchant Services, I was a Product Owner supporting a Pega-based application called MSOW. One of the primary workflows I supported was the Risk Profile Assessment, or RPA, which Credit Officers and Underwriters used to evaluate merchant risk.

Architecturally, MSOW functioned as the Pega UI and workflow layer. An upstream application called MSCD stored and processed merchant information and provided data and functionality through APIs that MSOW consumed.

Merchant information could originate from sources such as Fiserv and flow into MSCD. MSCD supported merchant-data storage and analysis used for periodic RPA reviews and other risk-related activity. MSOW then consumed the required information through APIs and presented it within the RPA case.

From there, the Credit Officer could review merchant information, analyze risk and exposure, gather additional information, work with supporting documents, consider collateral and the merchant's broader Wells Fargo relationship, and complete required concurrence or approval activities.

My responsibility as Product Owner was to understand those business and underwriting processes and translate them into requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, workflows, and prioritized backlog items . I worked with business stakeholders and development teams throughout Agile delivery and also supported UAT, defect resolution, release readiness, and production launch.
