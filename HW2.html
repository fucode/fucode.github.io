Honeywell makes complex physical products and automation systems. SAP S/4HANA is the digital backbone that turns product decisions into reliable data, materials, plans, costs, and orders. Your Product Owner job is likely to keep that chain aligned and deliverable.

Plain English: you probably will not design aircraft or factory controls. You will help design the business process and SAP capabilities that let Honeywell introduce, configure, manufacture, and sell products correctly.
S4 PO · Innovation to Order

01 / Company

Know which Honeywell you are joining

Honeywell’s structure changed in 2026. The current company named Honeywell Technologies is an automation business; Honeywell Aerospace and Solstice Advanced Materials are now separate public companies. The role’s exact employing entity and enterprise scope are worth confirming.

An industrial technology company built around operational outcomes

Honeywell Technologies sells hardware, software, controls, sensors, and services used in buildings, process industries, and industrial operations. Its value proposition centers on safety, productivity, efficiency, uptime, and the shift from automation toward more autonomous operations.

That explains why an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) transformation matters: the company’s product, supply, manufacturing, commercial, and finance processes must work across many businesses and locations without losing control or traceability.

Current-company note: Honeywell completed the Honeywell Aerospace spin-off on June 29, 2026. If interviewers use older segment language, listen for whether this S/4 program spans the pre-separation estate, the new Honeywell Technologies organization, transition-service obligations, or a specific business.

03 / Workstream

Innovation to Order (I2O), step by step

Honeywell’s exact process boundaries are not public in the role materials. This is the most likely end-to-end interpretation: move an approved product idea through governed product definition until commercial and operational systems can accept an order.

01

Idea & business case

Define customer problem, value, target market, and investment case.

Output: approved concept
02

New product introduction

Establish gates, owners, dates, requirements, and readiness criteria.

Output: NPI plan
03

Product definition

Create specifications, variants, engineering structure, and controlled changes.

Output: product design
04

Material & BOM setup

Create governed records for items, components, units, statuses, and relationships.

Output: trusted master data
05

Supply & production readiness

Confirm sourcing, planning, routings, capacity, quality, and plant extensions.

Output: executable supply
06

Commercial readiness

Establish sales views, configuration rules, pricing inputs, compliance, and costing.

Output: sellable offer
07

Order accepted

Create a valid order that flows downstream without manual repair.

Outcome: order-ready product

Where failure shows up

  • A missing material field blocks planning or procurement.
  • An incorrect bill of material drives the wrong components.
  • A local exception breaks the global process or an integration.
  • Engineering changes do not reach manufacturing or sales in time.
  • The happy path works, but approvals or error handling do not.

What success looks like

  • Product data is complete, valid, owned, and traceable.
  • Users can follow a standard process with clear exceptions.
  • Dependencies are visible before Program Increment (PI) commitment.
  • Acceptance covers process, data, controls, integration, and adoption.
  • Cycle time and rework improve—not just story completion.

What you will most likely be working on

The posting confirms backlog ownership, Program Increment Planning, SAP S/4HANA, Innovation-to-Order, material management, Jira, and cross-functional work. The exact modules, rollout wave, and authority model remain interview questions.

Work forecast

Own and prioritize the Team Backlog

Turn higher-level features into sequenced, team-ready work aligned to Product Management priorities.

Prepare for Program Increment (PI) Planning

Bring priorities, acceptance intent, dependencies, risks, and enough refinement for a credible plan.

Write functional requirements and acceptance criteria

Clarify business rules, scenarios, data impacts, controls, exceptions, and evidence of done.

Broker global-template versus local-process decisions

Test whether a request is a true legal or operational gap, a configuration choice, or a preference.

Support testing and rollout readiness

Shape User Acceptance Testing (UAT), resolve defects, confirm cutover criteria, and learn from hypercare.

Own named SAP modules or integrations

Likely touchpoints exist, but confirm the exact module, system integrator, business unit, and deployment phase.

A realistic rhythm

Refinement

Make work executable

Review stories with developers, functional consultants, architects, and testers; expose unanswered business decisions.

Stakeholder alignment

Make trade-offs explicit

Align process owners, product management, engineering, operations, and data owners around value and risk.

Dependency control

Protect the end-to-end flow

Track what another team, system, site, or data domain must provide—and by when.

Demonstration

Inspect real process behavior

Validate scenarios against acceptance criteria; capture feedback without turning every preference into scope.

Testing

Decide what is acceptable

Triage defects by business impact and decide whether evidence supports acceptance or further work.

PI events

Connect team delivery to ART outcomes

Plan, negotiate scope, surface risks, and align objectives across the Agile Release Train (ART).

06 / Interview

Seven questions that turn “potential” into exact scope

Use these to separate what the posting says from how the job actually works. The answers will reveal the module mix, transformation stage, authority, and near-term expectations.

Which legal entity and business segments does this role support now?Confirm the effect of the 2026 Honeywell Technologies and Honeywell Aerospace separation on scope.
Where does Honeywell define the start and end of Innovation to Order?Ask which NPI gates, engineering objects, material processes, and order-readiness outcomes are included.
Which SAP modules and surrounding platforms are in this team’s boundary?Listen for Product Lifecycle Management, Materials Management, Production Planning, Sales and Distribution, variant configuration, engineering, and data-governance tools.
Is this a greenfield design, a legacy conversion, a deployment wave, or post-go-live optimization?The work changes radically depending on program phase.
What decisions can this Product Owner make without escalation?Clarify Team Backlog priority, story acceptance, process policy, template exceptions, and release scope.
What is the hardest dependency or source of rework today?Probe for master data, interfaces, late business decisions, testing, environment readiness, or adoption.
What measurable outcome must improve in the first 90 days?Ask for cycle time, data quality, release predictability, defect escape, adoption, or business readiness—not output alone.
How do Honeywell and the system integrator divide accountability?Internal ownership should remain clear for business outcomes, priority, and acceptance.
